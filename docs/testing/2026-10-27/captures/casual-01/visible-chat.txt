Yes — that’s the concentric-blue diagram in the Microsoft New Future of Work Report (2025). Here’s the figure from the report:

Nearby caption (inferred): Opportunities for augmenting humans are far greater than opportunities to automate existing tasks (Adapted from Brynjolfsson, 2022). — New-Future-Of-Work-Report-2025.pdf (page 23)

What it shows (New-Future-Of-Work-Report-2025.pdf, page 23):

The smallest central circle is labeled "Human tasks that machines should automate."

A larger inner ring is labeled "Human tasks that machines could automate."

The largest outer circle is "Tasks that humans can do."

A pointer to the right labels "New tasks that humans can do with the help of machines."

So no — the "human tasks that machines should automate" area is smaller than the other human-task areas (it’s the smallest circle). The figure’s point (captioned “Opportunities for augmenting humans are far greater than opportunities to automate existing tasks”) is that augmentation/new tasks with machines is a much bigger opportunity than simply automating existing human tasks (New-Future-Of-Work-Report-2025.pdf, p.23).
